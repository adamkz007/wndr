Privacy

Clear privacy commitments for Wndr.

This page gives visitors a plain-language summary of how the website and product are intended to handle data before they download the app.

Local First

Your library content is meant to stay on your device by default, including imported documents, notes, tags, and related metadata.

No Forced Account

You do not need an online account to browse the site, inspect the repository, or understand the local-first positioning of the app.

Explicit Sharing

Exporting, sharing, or syncing should happen only when you intentionally trigger those actions through product features you control.

Website Data

The public website is a lightweight static site. Its purpose is to present product information, release links, and documentation without requiring sign-in.

  • The site does not describe any ad-tech requirement or behavioral profiling.
  • Downloads route through GitHub release infrastructure.
  • Any future analytics should be clearly disclosed and remain minimal.

Application Data

Wndr is positioned as a local-first research workspace. Based on the current product messaging and repository documentation, the app is designed to keep working materials close to the user.

  • Documents, notes, collections, tags, and metadata remain on your machine by default.
  • Cloud storage is not presented as a requirement for core usage.
  • Backups, exports, and automation actions should happen through explicit user intent.

Security Posture

Security details evolve with the product, but the guiding principle is to reduce unnecessary data movement and keep the trust surface small.

  • Local storage reduces reliance on third-party systems for everyday reading and note-taking.
  • The deeper technical reference remains in `docs/storage-and-security.md`.
  • Material privacy changes should be reflected both here and in the repository docs.

Your Choices

You can inspect the source, review the documentation, and decide whether Wndr fits your expectations before downloading it.

  • Read the storage and security documentation on GitHub.
  • Review the repository and release history.
  • Use GitHub to contact the maintainer with questions about data handling.
